Vastu comes up in roughly half our Indore projects. Sometimes it is the client's own conviction; often it is a parent's, and the client wants to honour it without living in a house that looks like a temple.

The good news: vastu is almost entirely about _where_ things go, not _what they look like_. A north-east pooja, a south-west master bedroom and a south-east kitchen can all be minimal, Scandinavian, mid-century or anything else. Here are the eight principles that come up most, and what each one actually asks of a flat layout.

Not one of these asks for a particular style. The arch, the carved jaali, the red and gold — those are cultural associations, not vastu requirements.

## What vastu does not touch

It is worth saying clearly: vastu has nothing to say about laminates vs veneer, false ceilings, lighting temperature, sofa shape, or whether your kitchen is handleless. Clients sometimes arrive believing the vastu list is longer than it is because a relative has added their own preferences to it. Separating the two early makes the whole project easier.

## A worked example: 3BHK in Scheme 78

The flat: 1,450 sq ft carpet, east-facing main door, kitchen in the south-east (good), master bedroom in the north-west (not ideal), pooja niche provided by the builder in the south (poor).

**What the family asked for:** vastu alignment per their consultant's notes, and a home that "looks like the photographs we save" — pale wood, white walls, cane, brass.

**What we changed:**

1. **Swapped the master and the second bedroom.** The second bedroom was in the south-west and slightly smaller. We gave it the full-height wardrobe wall and a hydraulic bed to compensate for the lost 20 sq ft. The parents now sleep in the SW with heads to the south.
2. **Closed the builder's pooja niche** and used it as a linen cupboard. Built a 30 in wall-mounted pooja unit on the north-east wall of the dining area — teak veneer, white marble base, CNC jaali backlit at 2700K.
3. **Rotated the living room.** The sofa moved to the south wall facing north, so the family sits facing the entrance and the light. The TV went on the north wall in a low oak unit. Open shelving on the east, closed storage on the south and west.
4. **Kitchen mitigation.** It was already in the SE. We put the hob on the east wall under the window and the sink on the north wall. The refrigerator went to the south-west corner of the kitchen.
5. **Mirrors.** The dresser mirror in the master went on the east wall, perpendicular to the bed. Wardrobe shutters are plain; one inside face carries a full-length mirror.
6. **Palette by direction.** Warm white (LRV 80) on the north and east walls throughout. A single deep terracotta on the south wall of the living room, behind the sofa. Sage on the west wall of the master.

**What it looks like:** oak laminate, white matte shutters, cane on the dining chairs and the TV unit, a brass pendant over the dining table, linen curtains. Nobody who walks in says "vastu house." The consultant signed it off in one visit.

## Where it gets difficult

- **Toilets in the north-east.** Common in older Indore buildings. Cannot be moved without a structural engineer and society permission. Mitigations are limited to keeping the door closed, lighter colours, and good ventilation. Be honest with yourself about whether this is acceptable before buying.
- **Kitchen in the north-east.** Same problem. Mitigation as in the table above. Some consultants are stricter than others; ask yours before signing.
- **Main door facing south-west.** Mitigations exist (threshold, light, specific colours) but this is the one placement where consultants disagree most.
- **Staircase in the centre** in duplexes. Nothing to be done; this is where we recommend accepting it or choosing a different flat.

<Callout tone="info" title="Get the brief in writing">
  Ask your vastu consultant for a one-page written note: directions for each room, bed head, hob,
  pooja, mirrors, colours. Hand it to your designer at the start. Verbal vastu advice delivered room
  by room during execution is the single biggest cause of rework we see.
</Callout>
