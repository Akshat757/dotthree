You asked three firms to quote for your flat. One sent a two-page PDF with a lump sum. One sent a 14-page Excel with 212 line items. One sent a WhatsApp message with "approx 9.5 to 11 lakh depending on material." The totals are ₹8.2 lakh, ₹11.6 lakh and "approx 10."

They are not comparable, and the firms know it. Here is how to make them comparable in an afternoon.

## Step 1: Put all three onto one sheet

Make a spreadsheet with one row per _room element_, not per line item. Use these rows — they match how we and most Indore firms actually build:

- Kitchen: base units, wall units, tall units, loft, countertop, backsplash, chimney and hob, accessories
- Each wardrobe: carcass and shutters, loft, internal fittings, mirror
- TV unit, crockery unit, pooja unit, study table, shoe unit — one row each
- Beds, side tables, dressers — one row each, marked "loose furniture"
- False ceiling — one row per room
- Electrical — points, wiring, fittings (separate rows)
- Painting
- Civil — demolition, tiling, plumbing, waterproofing
- Soft furnishing — curtains, blinds, upholstery
- Design fee, project management fee, GST, transport, deep cleaning

Now map every line of every quote into one of these rows. The lump-sum quote will leave most rows empty — that is already information.

## Step 2: Convert everything to the same unit

Modular work is priced per running foot (rft) or per square foot of shutter area. Both are legitimate, but a quote in sq ft of shutter for a 7 ft tall wardrobe is 7× the number of an rft quote.

Convert to **rft of carcass length, measured along the wall** for kitchens, wardrobes and lofts. If a quote gives sq ft, divide the sq ft total by the unit height in feet. If a quote gives a lump sum per unit, divide by the length.

## Step 3: Pin down the material grade

This is where the 40% hides. For each modular row, write down from each quote:

- **Plywood:** BWP / BWR / MR / commercial — and the brand. "ISI marked ply" means nothing by itself.
- **Shutter finish:** Laminate (brand and thickness, usually 0.8 or 1 mm), acrylic, PU paint, veneer, membrane.
- **Edge banding:** PVC 2 mm on all edges, or 0.8 mm on visible edges only.
- **Hardware:** Hinges and channels — brand and whether soft-close is included. Hettich, Häfele, Ebco, Blum are different price classes.
- **Countertop:** Granite (which one, and thickness), quartz (brand), or "as per selection" — which means not included.

If a quote does not say, email and ask. A firm that cannot answer "which plywood, which hinge" in one reply is going to decide on site, in its own favour.

<Callout tone="warning" title="The phrase to watch for">
  "Or equivalent." It lets the contractor substitute any brand at the same *price to them*, not the
  same quality to you. Ask for it to be struck out, or ask for the approved alternates to be named.
</Callout>

## Step 4: Find the exclusions

Read every footnote. Common exclusions that turn a cheap quote into an expensive one:

- Electrical wiring and points ("client scope")
- Painting ("client to arrange")
- Civil work: demolition, debris removal, tile work in bathrooms
- Countertop and sink ("as per selection")
- Chimney, hob, appliances
- Loose furniture (beds, sofas, dining)
- Curtains and blinds
- Transportation, scaffolding, deep cleaning
- GST (quoted as "extra as applicable")
- Design fee, 3D views, site visits beyond a number

Add a row for each and estimate the missing items from the reference table above. Now you have three real totals.

## Step 5: Compare the terms, not just the number

- **Payment schedule.** Front-loaded schedules (50%+ before any work) shift all the risk to you.
- **Timeline** with a start date and a handover date — and what happens if they miss it.
- **Warranty:** How many years, on what (hardware vs carcass vs finish), and who services it.
- **Change orders:** How are additions priced? At the same unit rates, or "as per actuals"?
- **Site supervision:** Who, how often, and is there a named project lead?
- **Drawings:** Do you get working drawings before production, and can you sign them off?

The cheapest quote was never really ₹8.2 lakh. It was ₹11.4 lakh with worse hardware and no named plywood.

## What a good quote looks like

It is itemised by room and element. It names brands. It states the unit and the quantity for every modular line. It includes GST. It lists what is excluded in plain words on page one, not page fourteen. And the number it arrives at is close to what [a neutral estimator](/estimate/) produces for the same tier.
