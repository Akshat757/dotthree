Half our renovation clients cannot move out. Rent in Indore for three months, moving twice, kids' school routines — it does not add up. So the work happens around the family.

It can be done well. It is never painless. This is the checklist we give families before we start, with the three trade-offs we ask them to accept up front.

## The three trade-offs

1. **It takes longer.** Sequential rooms instead of parallel trades. Budget 25–35% more calendar time than the empty-flat schedule.
2. **One room is always unusable.** Sometimes two. You will sleep in the living room at least once.
3. **Dust wins.** Sealing helps enormously; it does not eliminate. Fine dust reaches every surface at least twice during the project — after demolition and after sanding.

If any of these three is a deal-breaker, move out for the loud phase and come back for the finishing. Many families do a hybrid: two weeks at a relative's during civil and carcass installation, then back.

The principle: **finish and seal a room before opening the next**. The crew should never have two rooms in a dusty state at once.

## Dust control that actually works

- **Plastic sheeting on the door frame** of the work room, taped on all four sides, with a zip door. Not a bedsheet.
- **Negative pressure:** an exhaust fan in the work room's window blowing out, so air flows in from the house, not out into it.
- **Wet cutting** where possible, and a shop-vac on the sanders. Insist on this for the ceiling putty stage.
- **Floor protection:** corrugated sheet or 2 mm foam board on the entire path from the main door to the work room, taped at joints.
- **Daily sweep and wipe** of the common areas by the crew before leaving — write it into the contract.
- **Cover everything** in the adjacent rooms with plastic, including inside open shelves. Seal the AC indoor units.
- **Switch off the AC** in the work room — it pulls dust through the coil.

## Protect the kitchen first, even if it is last

The kitchen is the room that keeps a household functioning. Even when the kitchen is phase 3, from day one:

- Keep the door closed and a wet mat outside it.
- Cover the counters at the end of each day.
- Move open food to sealed boxes.

When the kitchen phase arrives, set up a temporary kitchen: an induction plate, a kettle, a small fridge if the main one is being moved, and a water point. The balcony or a corner of the dining area works. Plan for 12–16 days of this — budget for more takeaways than you think.

## Noise, timing and the society

The loudest work — tile breaking, core drilling for AC and chimney, slab drilling for the ceiling frame — should be grouped into as few days as possible and announced to neighbours in advance. Most Indore societies allow work 9 am to 6 pm on weekdays and until 1 pm on Saturdays.

A crew that respects this will be let back into the building for the snag visit. One that does not will have the watchman stopping deliveries by week two.

## Safety in an occupied flat

- Power tools unplugged and stored at the end of each day.
- No exposed wiring left overnight. Temporary boards with an MCB, not loose wires into a socket.
- Paints, thinners and adhesives stored in a closed box on the balcony, never in a bedroom.
- Stairs and ladders folded and laid flat overnight.
- A fire extinguisher in the flat for the duration — ₹1,500 well spent.

<Callout tone="tip" title="The one thing that saves the most frustration">
  Agree a **no-work room** from day one — one room that the crew never enters, where the family can
  sit without dust, noise or strangers. It changes the experience from an occupation to a project.
</Callout>

## What it costs extra

Protection sheeting, zip doors, floor board and daily cleaning for a 2BHK add ₹12,000–20,000 to the project. Supervision time adds a further 2–3%. Against the cost of three months' rent and two moves, it is almost always the cheaper option — just not the easier one.
