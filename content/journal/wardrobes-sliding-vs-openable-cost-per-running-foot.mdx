The wardrobe is the second-largest line in most Indore interior budgets after the kitchen, and the shutter type is the decision clients spend the longest on. Sliding looks sleeker in the showroom. Openable costs less and works better. Neither is always right.

Here is the actual comparison, with 2026 costs per running foot.

## What each one costs

A 7 ft wide, 7 ft tall wardrobe with loft, Standard tier:

- Openable: 7 × ₹2,200 + 7 × ₹950 = **₹22,050**
- Sliding: 7 × ₹2,800 + 7 × ₹950 = **₹26,250**

The ₹4,200 difference buys the track, the thicker shutters that a sliding system needs to stay flat, and the aluminium frame or profile handles. Across two bedrooms, that is ₹8,000–10,000 — about the cost of all the internal accessories you actually want.

## The real difference: access

An openable wardrobe gives you the **entire interior at once**. Both shutters open, you see every shelf and the full hanging rod.

A sliding wardrobe gives you **half or a third at a time**. Two shutters, one opening — you see 50%. Three shutters, 66% of the wardrobe is behind a door at any moment. This is the complaint nobody mentions in the showroom and everybody mentions after a year: "I can never see where anything is."

Sliding also loses you depth. The two tracks sit one behind the other and take 3–4 in from the interior. A 24 in carcass gives you 20 in of usable depth — too shallow for a hanger to sit square. Sliding wardrobes should be 26–28 in deep, which pushes the wardrobe further into the room.

## When sliding is the right answer

- **Clearance under 24 in.** If the bed, once placed, leaves less than 2 ft in front of the wardrobe wall, openable shutters will hit the bed or you will not be able to stand in front of an open door. This is the only compelling reason.
- **A wardrobe longer than 10 ft** where a wall of openable shutters starts to look like a corridor of doors. Three wide sliders read calmer.
- **Mirror shutters.** A full-height mirror on a sliding panel is safer and cheaper than on a hinged door.
- **Kids' rooms** where slammed hinged doors are a daily event — sliding with a soft-close track survives better.

## When openable is the right answer

- Everything else. Rooms with 24 in or more of clearance. Wardrobes under 10 ft. Anyone who wants to see their whole wardrobe at once.
- **Internal fittings.** Pull-out trouser racks, tie trays and shoe shelves work from the front and need the door out of the way — openable gives them room.
- **Repair.** A hinge is a ₹200 part replaced in ten minutes. A sliding roller is a ₹600–1,500 part and the shutter comes off to replace it.

<Callout tone="tip" title="The 24-inch test">
  Place the bed (or tape its outline on the floor). Measure from the wardrobe wall to the bed edge.
  24 in or more: openable. 18–24 in: sliding, or move the bed 6 in. Under 18 in: the wardrobe is on
  the wrong wall — redraw the room before choosing shutters.
</Callout>

## Hardware: where the budget actually matters

The shutter type gets the attention. The hardware determines whether the wardrobe is a pleasure or an irritation in year three.

**Openable:**

- Soft-close hinges (Hettich Sensys, Häfele, Ebco Slim-line are in rising order of cost). Standard tier should include these on every door.
- 3 hinges per shutter above 5 ft tall, not 2.
- Handles: profile (recessed aluminium) for a handleless look, or a 128–160 mm bar. Push-to-open is a false economy on wardrobes — it marks and sticks with humidity.

**Sliding:**

- Bottom-running tracks are more stable than top-hung for shutters over 6 ft tall. Both exist in soft-close.
- Shutter thickness: 25 mm minimum for a 7 ft slider or it warps and the shutters touch. Many Essential-tier sliders are 18 mm and this is why they bind by the first monsoon.
- Aluminium frame profiles around the shutter add rigidity and cost ₹300–500/rft; worth it on anything over 6 ft 6 in tall.

What to skip at Standard tier: tie and belt trays (use a drawer insert), pull-down hanging rods (fragile and awkward), and glass-fronted shutters in a dusty city.

## Finish: laminate, acrylic, PU, veneer

- **Laminate (1 mm)**: the default. Matte, suede and textured wood-grain finishes are available at the same price; high gloss shows every fingerprint.
- **Acrylic**: the high-gloss, mirror-like finish. Premium tier. Scratches more easily than it looks; do not use in kids' rooms.
- **PU paint**: a sprayed lacquer finish, matte or gloss, any colour. Premium to Luxury. Beautiful and repairable; expensive.
- **Veneer**: real wood. Luxury tier for a whole wardrobe; often used on just the master wardrobe as the room's feature.

A trick we use often: laminate shutters in a matte colour with a single veneer or fluted panel bay at the end, nearest the door. It reads as a veneer wardrobe for a third of the price. ## A note on measuring

Running foot is the length of the carcass along the wall, not the wall length. A 10 ft wall with a 7 ft wardrobe is 7 rft. Lofts are measured the same way. Heights above 7 ft 6 in (to the ceiling) are quoted as wardrobe + loft, not as a taller wardrobe — the split is usually at 7 ft so that the loft is a separate box that can be installed last.

If a quote gives a lump sum per wardrobe, divide by the length and compare it to the table above — [here is the method](/journal/how-to-compare-three-interior-quotations/).
