The complaint we hear most in Indore flats is not "it looks dated." It is "there is nowhere to put anything." A 2BHK handed over by a builder has a kitchen platform, maybe one shelf in a bathroom, and nothing else. Everything a family of four owns — winter razai, suitcases, Diwali thalis, the pressure cooker you only use for parties — has to go somewhere.

Here are the ten storage moves we specify most, ranked roughly by cubic feet gained per rupee spent. The numbers are from our 2026 price book.

## 1. Lofts — the cheapest volume in the house

A loft is a cabinet above the wardrobe or kitchen wall units, running to the ceiling. In a 10 ft room above a 7 ft wardrobe, that is nearly 3 ft of height — about 12 cubic feet per running foot.

At ₹950/rft (Standard tier), a 7 ft wardrobe loft costs ₹6,650 and holds two suitcases and all the winter bedding. Nothing else on this list comes close.

**Do it right:** Lift-up or sliding shutters, not hinged — a hinged loft door at 8 ft is awkward to open and closes on your head. Match the wardrobe finish so the wall reads as one piece.

## 2. Hydraulic storage bed

A box bed with a gas-lift mechanism that raises the mattress platform. The entire footprint of a queen bed — 60 × 78 in × 12 in deep — becomes storage: around 32 cubic feet.

Cost: ₹28,000–45,000 for a Standard-tier bed with the mechanism, against ₹18,000–30,000 for a plain box bed with side drawers. The side-drawer version gives you a third of the volume and needs clearance to pull out, which small rooms do not have.

**Do it right:** Specify a 25 mm plywood platform (not 18 mm — it bows) and a mechanism rated for the mattress weight. Use it for seasonal items; it is a bad place for things you need weekly.

## 3. The 4-inch-deeper wardrobe

Standard wardrobe depth is 22–24 in. Make it 26–28 in and every shelf gains a stack of folded clothes, every hanger section gains the ability to hold winter jackets without crushing. Cost is almost nothing — the material increment is 10–15% on the carcass — but it must be planned before the room layout is fixed because you lose those inches from the floor.

## 4. Kitchen tall unit with internal pull-outs

The single most valuable upgrade in a small kitchen. A 2 ft wide, 7 ft tall unit beside the fridge with four or five pull-out wire baskets replaces a pantry you do not have. It holds a month of groceries at eye level, and it replaces three wall cabinets you cannot reach.

Cost: ₹22,000–35,000 at Standard tier including the baskets. Put the microwave and OTG in it at counter height and you free up two feet of counter.

## 5. Under-stair or under-window storage

If you have a duplex or a flat with a window sill at 2 ft 6 in or lower, the space below the window is dead. A bench seat with lift-up storage at 18 in deep gives you a window seat (which everybody uses) and 6–8 cubic feet per running foot of storage for books, toys or shoes.

Cost: ₹2,400–3,200/rft with a cushion. Beloved by kids and by anyone with a cat.

## 6. Shoe storage at the right depth

Shoe racks fail because they are too shallow (shoes stick out) or too tall (nobody bends to the bottom). The right spec: 14 in deep, 42 in tall, with angled pull-down flaps or plain shelves at 7 in centres. A 3 ft wide unit holds 18–24 pairs.

Cost: ₹14,000–22,000 at Standard tier. Put a seat on top and it becomes the foyer. In flats where the entrance opens directly into the living room, a tall unit (7 ft) with shoes at the bottom and a closed section for keys, helmets and bags above is a wall you actually need — see [our foyer guide](/rooms/foyer-entrance/).

## 7. Vertical dividers in the kitchen

A 4 in wide vertical tray slot next to the hob for thalis, baking trays and cutting boards. A 6 in pull-out for bottles. These replace the "stack it all in the corner cabinet" habit and cost ₹2,500–5,000 each as part of the kitchen.

## 8. The wall behind the door

Every bedroom door swings against a wall that stays empty. A 6 in deep, floor-to-ceiling open shelf unit behind the door — for books, a mirror, hooks for the next day's clothes — adds storage with zero footprint penalty. Cost: ₹8,000–15,000 in laminate.

## 9. Bathroom mirror cabinet and niche

Mirror cabinets replace the mirror with a 4–5 in deep cabinet behind it: toiletries, medicines, razors. A tiled wall niche in the shower at 12 × 24 in holds the shampoos that otherwise live on the floor. Both need planning before tiling. Cost: ₹6,000–12,000 for the cabinet; the niche is ₹2,500–4,000 of extra civil and tiling.

## 10. Utility balcony loft

The balcony where the washing machine lives has a ceiling. A 2 ft deep loft across the full width, in exterior-grade material (WPC board or marine ply with a PU finish), takes the buckets, the ladder, the spare gas cylinder and the ironing board.

Cost: ₹1,400–1,900/rft because of the exterior-grade material. Still cheaper than any wardrobe.
