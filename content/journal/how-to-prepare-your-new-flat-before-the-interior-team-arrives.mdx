Possession day is a celebration. The weeks after it are the most valuable time in the whole project — and the most often wasted. A flat that arrives for interior work with its builder defects raised, its services checked and its paperwork done saves two weeks and several arguments.

Here is what to do between the keys and the first delivery.

## First: wait, if you can

A new building moves. Plaster cures, joints settle, the flat above fills its bathroom for the first time. Most seepage, hairline cracking and tile hollowness shows up in the first six to eight weeks, and almost all of it shows up in the first monsoon.

If your possession is in April and you can wait until August to start interiors, do. If you cannot, close the flat for four to six weeks, run every tap and flush for ten minutes once a week, and go back with a torch.

## The twelve checks

Photograph everything, including the meter readings and every defect. Date the photographs. This is your defect record and your proof later.

## Raise builder defects in writing

Everything on that list that fails is the builder's to fix under the defect liability period. Raise it as one consolidated email with photographs, to the builder's customer-care address, and ask for a date. Do this _before_ any interior work begins. Once a wardrobe is standing against a seeping wall, the builder's position becomes "your contractor damaged it."

In Indore the most common items builders actually fix without argument are seepage, hollow tiles and window seals. Floor level and wall plumb are harder; be prepared to absorb those as civil work in your interior budget.

## Paperwork and access

- **Society NOC** for interior work. Most Indore societies have a standard form; it takes three to seven days.
- **Lift permission and deposit** — ₹5,000–25,000, refundable, against damage. Get it before the first material delivery.
- **Working hours** in writing. Share them with the interior firm.
- **Debris disposal:** where, how often, and who pays for the tempo. Civil waste cannot go in the society bins.
- **Electricity and water** active in your name. Site work cannot start on a temporary connection.
- **A key arrangement.** Who opens the flat each morning, and a spare with the project lead.

## What to leave, what to remove

Builders often hand over with a few fittings: basic lights, fans, a kitchen platform, sometimes a loft or a modular kitchen in "semi-furnished" flats. Decide early:

- **Builder kitchen:** Usually not worth keeping unless it is from a national brand and matches the layout you want. Removing it costs ₹3,000–6,000; building around it compromises the new kitchen.
- **Builder fans and lights:** Keep the fans if decent — they are rarely the problem. Remove the light fittings; they will be replaced by the lighting plan anyway.
- **Platform and sink:** The granite platform is often reusable. The sink almost never is.
- **Bathroom fittings:** Keep unless you are changing the tiles. Changing a CP fitting later is easy; changing tiles is not.

## The handover to your interior team

On the day before services start, hand the project lead:

1. Your defect record with photographs and the builder's response.
2. The electrical load letter and the DB photograph.
3. The as-built services drawing, or the scan results.
4. The society NOC, working hours and the lift permission.
5. Meter readings and the key.

A flat that arrives like this starts on time. Most do not, and that is where the first week of the "45-day" promise disappears — see [where the days actually go](/journal/how-long-interior-work-actually-takes/).
