False ceilings are the most over-prescribed item in Indian interiors. They appear on almost every quote because they are easy to sell — a photograph of a cove-lit ceiling looks expensive — and because they are high-margin. Sometimes they are exactly right. Often they are ₹60,000 of gypsum that lowers your room and gathers dust.

Here is how we decide, and what each option costs in Indore in 2026.

## The four real reasons to build a false ceiling

1. **You need to hide something.** AC ducting, a beam that cuts across the room, exposed conduit from a rewiring job, or the plumbing of the flat above in a bathroom. This is the original purpose and still the best one.
2. **You want layered lighting.** Spotlights, profile lights and coves need a cavity. If your lighting plan has more than two sources per room, a ceiling is how you get them without surface-mounted fittings.
3. **The slab is bad.** Many Indore flats built between 2008 and 2016 have slabs with 20–40 mm of undulation. Painting them flat is impossible; a ceiling is cheaper than skim-coating.
4. **You want to define zones** in an open living-dining — a drop over the dining table or a tray over the seating area changes how a 20 ft room reads without walls.

And the two reasons that usually do not hold up:

- _"It looks premium."_ Only if the room has the height to lose. In a 9 ft 3 in room, a full ceiling at 8 ft 6 in makes the room feel like a corridor.
- _"It keeps the room cooler."_ A little. A ceiling fan on the exposed slab does more.

The Standard gypsum rate of ₹120/sq ft is what the [estimator](/estimate/) uses. For a typical 2BHK living room of 220 sq ft, that is ₹26,400 for the ceiling itself, plus ₹8,000–15,000 for the lights that go into it.

## Gypsum vs PoP, properly

**Gypsum board** comes in 12.5 mm sheets screwed to a galvanised steel frame hung from the slab. Joints are taped and filled. It is fast (a living room in two to three days), dead flat, and the frame is easy to inspect.

**PoP** is plaster applied over a chicken-mesh and frame, or cast in moulds. It is slower, messier, and more dependent on the applicator's skill — but it can do a curve or a cornice that gypsum cannot without expensive pre-formed sections.

In Indore, the "PoP ceiling" in many quotes is actually gypsum board with PoP used for the jointing. Ask which it is. For a flat tray or a cove, gypsum is the right answer. For a curved bulkhead or a classical moulded design, PoP.

<Callout tone="warning" title="The frame matters more than the board">
  Insist on a GI (galvanised iron) frame of 0.5 mm or thicker with channels at 600 mm centres and
  hangers at 1,200 mm. The cheap version uses thinner sections at 900 mm centres, and the ceiling
  will sag and crack at the joints within three years. This is the single most common false-ceiling
  failure we are asked to repair.
</Callout>

## Height: the decision that cannot be undone

Measure your finished slab height (floor tile to underside of slab) before anything else.

- **Above 10 ft:** Build whatever the design calls for. A 6–8 in drop is barely noticeable.
- **9 ft 6 in to 10 ft:** A full ceiling is fine at a 4–6 in drop, which is the minimum for recessed spotlights and a GI frame. Skip bulky tray designs.
- **9 ft to 9 ft 6 in:** Peripheral cove only. Keep the centre at full height.
- **Below 9 ft:** No false ceiling. Use surface-mounted track lights or a well-chosen pendant and spend the money on paint and curtains.

Most Indore apartments from the last decade are between 9 ft 6 in and 10 ft. That is why the peripheral cove is our most-built ceiling type — it gives you the concealed lighting and hides the AC pipe run without taking height from the centre of the room.

## Lighting: the part that justifies the ceiling

A false ceiling without a lighting plan is wasted money. For a living room, a plan that works:

- **Ambient:** A cove or profile light around the perimeter, dimmable, 3000K warm white. This is the light you live in.
- **Task:** Two or three adjustable spotlights (10–12 W) over the seating and the TV wall, on a separate switch.
- **Accent:** One wall-washer or picture light on the art or feature wall.
- **Fan:** Yes, keep the fan. Plan its position before the frame goes up — a fan box needs a direct fixing to the slab, not to the gypsum.

Budget ₹35,000–60,000 for the fittings in a living room at Standard tier. Lights are where cheap and good look identical in the showroom and completely different after 18 months — buy drivers from a brand that will still exist to honour the warranty.

## Rooms, one by one

**Living room:** The most justified ceiling in the house, for lighting and zoning. Cove or shallow tray.

**Master bedroom:** Only if you have the height and want the lighting. A 2 ft wide drop above the headboard wall with two reading spots is a good compromise — ₹12,000–18,000 instead of ₹35,000.

**Kids' room:** Skip it unless hiding an AC pipe. Kids' rooms get re-done every eight years; a ceiling locks the layout.

**Kitchen:** A simple flat gypsum ceiling to hide the chimney duct and give you four good spotlights over the counter. Keep it 100% flat — grease settles on every ledge.

**Bathrooms:** PVC or moisture-resistant gypsum (green board). Needed to hide the plumbing of the flat above and to mount an exhaust fan properly.

**Pooja room:** A small drop with a concealed cove is one of the best ₹8,000 you can spend; see [our pooja room guide](/journal/how-to-design-a-pooja-room-in-a-modern-flat/).
